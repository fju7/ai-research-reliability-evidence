# R1 independent reconstruction

**Disposition:** `R1_CHARACTERIZATION_INCORRECT`

**Review date:** 2026-09-15 (America/New_York)

## Question tested

The draft alleged that source evidence described adverse events in a combination-treatment arm, WHU changed that into attribution to one component, and a correction process then added authority to the changed claim.

## Source-native reconstruction

The relevant trial was KEYNOTE-942/mRNA-4157-P201, ClinicalTrials.gov `NCT03897881`: intismeran autogene (mRNA-4157/V940) plus pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone. The public five-year sponsor release dated 1 June 2026 reports two distinct safety propositions in sequence:

1. The most common adverse events “attributed to intismeran autogene in combination with KEYTRUDA” were fatigue 59.6%, injection-site pain 59.6%, and chills 51.0%.
2. The majority of adverse events “attributed to intismeran autogene” were Grade 1 (31.7%) or Grade 2 (51.9%).

The first phrase is grammatically awkward and can be read as referring to the combination regimen. The second is unambiguously component-specific. The study structure does not, by itself, identify causality for a component because there was no intismeran-alone arm; the component attribution is an investigator/sponsor treatment-relatedness judgment, not a randomized contrast isolating intismeran.

## Repository chain

- Earlier WHU prose: “Most side effects were mild to moderate — fatigue 59.6%, injection-site pain 59.6%, chills 51.0%.”
- Change record, 28 August 2026: “Most of the side effects attributed to the vaccine were mild to moderate …”
- Adjudication record: the correction was made after rereading the public release and explicitly relied on its component-specific Grade 1/2 sentence.
- Public correction narrative later said the source attributed the events “to the vaccine specifically.” That summary compressed the source and failed to preserve the important distinction between sponsor/investigator attribution and causal isolation by trial design.

## Finding

The essay’s R1 narrative omitted the source’s adjacent, explicit component-attribution sentence. The WHU correction therefore did not demonstrably transform combination-arm observation into unsupported single-component attribution. The stronger defensible criticism is narrower: WHU’s correction narrative overstated what the trial design could establish about component causality. That is not the chain the essay claimed.

R1 is removed from the essay rather than qualified into a vivid but ambiguous example.
