# Technical Brief: Who Checks the Research When the User Cannot?

**Status:** near-sendable internal draft after external-agenda mapping; not sent.
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-09-16.

## Problem

A sophisticated generalist may be able to inspect a cited source but be unable to reproduce a domain expert's search, applicability judgments, evidence weighting and final synthesis. We ask what must be demonstrated before such a user can rationally rely on a machine-managed report without routine domain-expert verification.

This is not a claim that current systems usually fail. WHU's historical corpus is too incomplete to establish its own past residuals (S28). The motivation instead comes from a conjunction: strong bounded systems now automate meaningful research components (S05–S09, S15–S19), while independent and primary evidence still shows bounded judgment, composition, correlated-evaluator and reference limitations (S13, S20–S22).

## State of the art

Professional engineering can establish exact object identity, native custody, lineage, execution integrity, invalidation, correction and authority boundaries. Persistent retrieval and domain-specific workflows improve acquisition and synthesis. Structured professional methods address search completeness, bias, indirectness and certainty (S02–S04). Debate, critique, competing hypotheses and sensitivity can expose errors or fragile assumptions in bounded settings (S22, S26).

None of these facts establishes arbitrary open-domain report warrant. Current public evaluations terminate epistemic load in expert rubrics, protected banks, closed corpora, executable tests, experiments, later outcomes, model judges calibrated against humans, or users. Current public evidence does not show one system satisfying acquisition adequacy, semantic fidelity, weighting/applicability, global inference, alternatives/defeaters, calibration and coverage together for consequential arbitrary-domain use.

This is active prior art, not a problem unique to WHU. OpenAI calls closely related work scalable oversight, critique assistance and monitorability. Anthropic studies weak-to-strong supervision and explicitly distinguishes outcome-gradable automated research from broader non-outcome-gradable work. METR studies task validity, weak monitors, reward hacking and manual evaluation burden. The UK AI Security Institute describes hard-to-supervise “fuzzy” automated-alignment tasks in which plausible errors can aggregate into compelling false safety assessments. Deep-research benchmarks use expert reports and thousands of fine-grained rubrics to evaluate open-ended reports. Professional evidence synthesis and assurance already structure search, applicability, uncertainty, defeaters and intended use.

Our claim is therefore not a new scalable-oversight problem. It is that open-domain evidence synthesis is a hard application region where the usual reference often cannot be supplied cheaply at runtime.

## Verification coverage and weak supervision

We use `verification coverage` as integrative shorthand, not a novelty claim. It restates established concerns in specification validation/completeness, partial oracles, assurance defeaters, construct validity and proxy misspecification (S01, S25). A verifier may accurately establish citation support while the report depends on unchecked evidence completeness, applicability or bridge premises. The weak-supervisor asymmetry is that the user is least able to check those residual judgments.

The proposed seven dimensions are audit lenses, not independent stages. Shared missing evidence can affect representation, weighting, alternatives and confidence; a wrong applicability model can contaminate both synthesis and its verifier. End-to-end procedures may satisfy several requirements jointly.

## Reliance requirements

A defensible intended-use claim needs: native provenance; evidence-route visibility and insufficiency handling; authority/timeliness/applicability; semantic fidelity; dependence control; diagnostic weighting; global/bridge warrant; alternatives/defeaters; uncertainty and underdetermination; calibrated authority and normative separation; correction/liveness; execution-path integrity; and evidence that validation covers the load-bearing properties.

For expert-free runtime reliance, protected independent evaluation must demonstrate low consequential false assurance under relevant domain, source, modality and temporal shifts; boundary recognition or safe escalation; acceptable expert/latency burden; and robustness to model/control drift. LLM self-judgment, same-model judging, citation accuracy, or correlated-model majority vote do not suffice alone (S21).

## Strongest counterarguments

1. This is established epistemology, assurance and scalable oversight under new vocabulary. We agree on the components; the claimed value is an intended-use crosswalk, not conceptual novelty.
2. Experts disagree and are imperfect. Therefore references should preserve disagreement and acceptable conclusion sets; it does not follow that unvalidated machine substitution is adequate.
3. Protected validation may suffice without per-report experts. This is the most credible path and would count as success if boundary recognition, drift and audit are demonstrated.
4. Proprietary systems may already have stronger controls. They may; the conclusion is restricted to public evidence.
5. General reliance is user- and stakes-specific. Correct; thresholds must be declared per intended use.
6. The right solution may remain routine expert review. If so, WHU may still improve auditability, but its intended generalist substitution is not demonstrated.

## Prospective observation

Inactive PR #30 proposes consecutive observation after WHU's professional baseline. It can identify which residual mechanisms survive, where they first appear, whether user-visible controls expose them and what correction costs follow. It cannot establish prevalence across AI systems, prove a general solution or manufacture an expert reference.

## Candidate nonredundant questions

1. Can a system recognize when a research task lies outside its validated domain/source/modality/dependency envelope with acceptably low false assurance and escalation burden?
2. Under protected expert reference and matched cost, does candidate-blind alternative synthesis improve materially distinct steelmanning and correct updating over strong candidate-conditioned critique?
3. Which consequential residual mechanisms survive a professionally engineered workflow in consecutive naturalistic use, and are they observable before release?

## What is prior art and what may add value

The reliance requirements, structured judgment, intended-use validation, selective audit, alternatives and specification-versus-verification distinction are prior art. “Verification coverage” is shorthand for their intersection, not a novelty claim. Potential value lies in a crosswalk tied to one declared end use; explicit accounting of expert labor moved upstream versus required at runtime; and prospective naturalistic measurement of false-green, overstrong reports, boundary failures and correction paths after professional engineering is fixed.

Strong falsifiers are: a current public system demonstrating low consequential false assurance across a declared cross-domain envelope without routine expert review; reliable boundary recognition with acceptable burden under shift; a simpler expert-assisted workflow dominating the proposed process; or PR #30 yielding no stable residual mechanism or no defensible reference.

## Requested critique

We want criticism of: whether this is merely scalable oversight or assurance engineering with new vocabulary; whether the open-domain/reference distinction is operational; missing programs or results; whether the requirements are overcomplete or wrongly coupled; defensible false-assurance/burden endpoints; viable imperfect-reference designs; and whether prospective WHU data could answer anything worth knowing. We are not asking for endorsement, collaboration, data, access or deployment.
